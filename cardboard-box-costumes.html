Cardboard Box Costume Ideas

The box your last delivery came in is a costume waiting to happen. A medium box worn over the torso becomes a robot, a pizza slice, a game block, or a dinosaur body with nothing more than a box cutter, tape, and paint.

Ask a grocery or liquor store for spares if you need big ones, they give them away free most mornings. Cut arm and head holes smaller than you think, reinforce inside edges with packing tape so they survive the night, and keep paint to one or two coats so the cardboard stays light.

Every costume below starts from a box, lists the rest of the supplies honestly, and links to a full build guide with real hands-on time. The box is already in the recycling. The costume is not far behind.

Pizza Slice

⏱ 45 min hands-onMedium

You need: Large cardboard box, 1; Yellow and orange acrylic paint, 1 bottle each; Red felt, 1 sheet; Brown felt scraps for crust, or 1 brown paper bag; Paintbrush, scissors, tape, 1 set; Fabric glue, 1 tube.

A big cardboard triangle, painted golden with felt pepperoni.

How to make it

  1. Cut a big triangle from the cardboard, about 3 feet tall.
  2. Paint it golden yellow-orange. An adult cuts red felt circles, and you glue them on spread out as pepperoni.
  3. Glue a brown crust strip along the wide end, tape two string loops to the back, and wear it like a sandwich board, crust at the top.

Donut

⏱ 35 min hands-onEasy

You need: 1 large cardboard box, about 24x24 inches flat; Pink paint, 1 small bottle; Felt sprinkles, 12 to 15 small pieces, or sticker sprinkles; Ribbon or string, 4 feet; Scissors, 1 pair and glue, 1 bottle.

Pink cardboard ring with sprinkles, worn like a sandwich board.

How to make it

  1. Draw a big circle on the cardboard, about 30 inches across. Cut the center hole smaller than the head first, then widen it slowly, keeping fingers clear of the scissors.
  2. Paint the ring pink.
  3. Glue on felt or sticker sprinkles, attach ribbon at the top, and wear it like a sandwich board.

Kart Racers

⏱ 45 min hands-onMedium

You need: Large cardboard box, 1 per racer, big enough to fit around the torso; Red, blue, green, and yellow paint or wrapping paper, 1 bottle or roll per color; Paper plates for wheels, 4 per kart; Racing cap or helmet, 1 per person; Scissors, 1 pair, tape, 1 roll, and string, 1 spool; Ribbon for kart straps, about 3 feet per kart.

Cardboard karts and racing caps for a full starting grid of friends.

How to make it

  1. Have each racer step into their box and mark the waist before cutting. Cut the top and bottom out small first, then widen.
  2. Paint each kart a different bright color, and tape two paper plate wheels to each side.
  3. Tape ribbon straps inside so the kart hangs at waist height with legs free to move. Tape every cut edge smooth, and wear the kart with a racing cap.

Ghost Hunters

⏱ 30 min hands-onEasy

You need: 1 khaki or tan jumpsuit, or matching shirt and pants, per person; 1 small cardboard box per person, shoebox size; White paper, 2 sheets, and markers for name patches; 1 sheet black cardboard per person; Packing tape or glue, scissors.

Khaki jumpsuits, cardboard ghost-catching backpacks, name patches.

How to make it

  1. Tape a small cardboard box shut for each backpack. An adult cuts the straps, dial, and hose from black cardboard.
  2. Tape or glue those pieces onto the box.
  3. Write each person's last name on white paper and tape it to the chest as a name patch. Wear the backpack over a khaki jumpsuit, and keep the box small and light.

Popcorn Bucket

⏱ 30 min hands-onEasy

You need: Cardboard box, 1 medium; Red and white striped wrapping paper or paint, 1 roll; Yellow or white balloons, 8 to 10; Tape, string, scissors, 1 set; Red marker for the label, 1.

A striped cardboard-box body with balloon popcorn on top.

How to make it

  1. An adult cuts the head and arm holes small first, then widens them to fit. Tape the inside edges so the cardboard does not scratch.
  2. Cover the box with red and white stripes, using striped paper or paint, and add the label with the red marker.
  3. Blow up the balloons and tie them in a cluster on top of the box, then step in. Keep balloons whole and away from toddlers.

Block Game Crew

⏱ 45 min hands-onMedium

You need: 1 large cardboard box per person, big enough to fit over the torso; Acrylic paint in each hero's colors, 1 small bottle per color; Paintbrushes, 1 wide and 1 detail per person; Black marker, 1; Scissors, 1 pair.

Cardboard-box heads: pick your blocky hero.

How to make it

  1. Each person picks a blocky game hero. An adult measures each head and cuts the head opening and eye holes wide, so everyone can see clearly.
  2. Paint each box in that hero's main colors.
  3. Draw a square face and details on the front with the black marker, and wear the boxes over regular clothes.

Cardboard Robot Crew

⏱ 45 min hands-onMedium

You need: Medium cardboard box per person, 1 each; Small cardboard box per person for the head, 1 each; Aluminum foil, 1 large roll; Plastic bottle caps, about 20 total; Duct tape, 1 roll; Black permanent marker, 1; Elastic string or yarn, 6 feet; Glow sticks, 1 per person.

Boxy robots built from cardboard boxes, foil, and bottle-cap buttons.

How to make it

  1. Cut head and arm holes in each big box, and wide eye holes in each small head box. Cut slowly and keep fingers clear of the scissors.
  2. Wrap every box in foil and tape it down. Draw dials on the chest, and tape bottle caps beside them as buttons.
  3. Slide into the torso box and tie the head box on with the chin string loose. Tape a cracked glow stick to the chest as a power light.

Plug and Socket

⏱ 45 min hands-onEasy

You need: Cardboard box, 1 large; Gray and white acrylic paint, 1 small bottle each; Black markers, 1 set; Craft foam, 1 sheet 9x12 inches; Tape, 1 roll, and string, 1 spool, for wearing; Scissors, 1 pair.

Cardboard plug and outlet worn front and back.

How to make it

  1. Cut two cardboard panels, each about 2 feet square.
  2. Paint one gray and add foam prongs for the plug. Paint the other white and draw dark slots for the socket.
  3. Tape string to the back corners and hang the panels like signs. Never loop string around the neck.

Haunted Animatronics

⏱ 60 min hands-onMedium

You need: 1 large cardboard box per person, big enough to cover the head; 2 red LED tea lights per person; Paint in gray, black, and one accent color, 1 small bottle each; 1 set old clothes with holes or tears; Hot glue gun, 1; scissors, 1 pair; utility knife, 1 (adults only).

Glitchy mascot heads from cardboard boxes, flickering LED eyes, jerky moves.

How to make it

  1. An adult marks the eye holes and the wide jagged mouth, checks the eye spots while the wearer holds the box up, then cuts both wide. Cardboard cannot be put back.
  2. Paint the box gray with black scuffs and cracks. An adult glues one red LED tea light inside each eye hole with the hot glue gun.
  3. Wear the box over old clothes and move in short jerky steps. Walk with a partner who can see clearly, never wear the box near stairs or traffic, and take the box off if you feel hot.

Party Pinata

⏱ 40 min hands-onMedium

You need: Cardboard box, 1 large; Crepe paper in rainbow colors, 6 rolls; Wrapped candy, 2 bags; String for hanging strips, 1 roll; Tape, 1 roll; Scissors, 1 pair.

A cardboard box wrapped in rainbow fringe, with real candy inside.

How to make it

  1. Cut fringe strips from the crepe paper, and glue them in overlapping rows until the whole box is covered.
  2. Cut arm and head holes, keeping the head hole generous so seeing and breathing stay easy. Leave an opening at the bottom.
  3. Fill the box with wrapped candy only, seal the bottom flap loosely, and hand out candy from inside the costume all night.

Fairy Tale Princesses

⏱ 15 min hands-onEasy

You need: 1 dress from the closet per person; 1 cereal box per crown; Gold or silver paint or markers, 1 set; Scissors, 1 pair and tape, 1 roll; 1 hair ribbon per person.

A dress or a crown from the closet. Every princess works.

How to make it

  1. Each princess picks a dress from her own closet.
  2. An adult draws a crown on a cereal box, checks the fit against the head, and cuts it out, leaving a 1 inch tab to tape the ends together.
  3. Color the crown gold or silver, tape it on, and tie the hair ribbon in a bow under the crown.

Good Witch, Bad Witch

⏱ 20 min hands-onEasy

You need: 1 black dress or set of black clothes for the bad witch; 1 pink dress or set of pink clothes for the good witch; Green face paint, non-toxic, 1 tube; 1 cardboard crown; 1 black cone hat; Hot glue gun, scissors, 1 pink marker, tape.

Green face paint and black for one, pink gown and crown for the other.

How to make it

  1. Bad witch: wear black, and paint the face and hands green with non-toxic paint. Keep the paint away from the eyes, and patch test sensitive skin first.
  2. An adult folds the black hat brim and hot glues it in place. Hot glue sets in seconds, so check the fit in a mirror first.
  3. Good witch: wear pink, color the cardboard crown with the pink marker, and tape it on.

Questions, answered

Where do I get big cardboard boxes for free?

Ask grocery or liquor stores in the morning; they give away sturdy boxes free most days. Appliance boxes from a buy-nothing group are the best fit for kid torsos.

How do I stop the cardboard tearing at the arm holes?

Reinforce every cut edge on the inside with packing tape before the costume goes on. Cut holes smaller than you think and widen slowly; cardboard cut away cannot go back.

What paint works on cardboard?

Acrylic craft paint or spray paint, one or two thin coats. Thick coats add weight and make the box sag by the end of the night.

Is a cardboard box costume safe for little kids?

Have an adult do all box-cutter work and tape every inside edge so raw cardboard never scratches skin. Keep the costume away from open flames; cardboard is flammable.
