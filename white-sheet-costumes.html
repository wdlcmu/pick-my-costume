White Sheet Costume Ideas

That old bedsheet in the linen closet is the fastest costume in the house. A white flat sheet, scissors, and a marker get you the classic ghost in ten minutes flat, and a black sheet cut into a cape covers vampires, headless horsemen, and little witches with almost no sewing.

The trick most people miss is sizing the sheet before you cut: twin for kids under eight, full or queen for teens and adults, and always cut eye holes smaller than you think, you can widen them but you cannot shrink them.

Every costume below starts from a sheet you already own, lists exactly what else you need, gives the build steps right on this page, and links to a full build guide with real hands-on time. No costume-shop run required.

Classic Ghost

⏱ 10 min hands-onEasy

You need: 1 white flat sheet: twin size for kids under 8, full size for tweens and adults; Black marker, thick tip, 1; Scissors, 1 pair; 2 small squares of black tulle or sheer fabric, about 4 inches each (optional, for see-through eyes; buy: craft store).

A white sheet with cut-out eyes.

How to make it

  1. Drape the sheet over the wearer and mark both eye spots.
  2. Take the sheet off. Cut the holes small first, then widen a little at a time.
  3. Draw a mouth, trim the hem so it clears the ground, and drape the sheet back on.

Little Witch

⏱ 15 min hands-onEasy

You need: Black cape or black bedsheet, 1; Pointy witch hat, 1; Black-and-white striped tights, 1 pair; Green non-toxic face paint, 1 tube; Safety pin or ribbon, 1.

Black cape, pointy hat, striped tights, green face paint.

How to make it

  1. Pin or tie the black cape at the shoulders, loose at the neck.
  2. Paint the nose, chin, and forehead lightly green. Use non-toxic face paint only, and keep it clear of the eyes.
  3. Pull on the striped tights and the pointy hat.

Headless Horsemen

⏱ 20 min hands-onEasy

You need: Black cape or black sheet, 1 per person; Small jack-o-lantern bucket or foam pumpkin, 1 per person; Broom or stick-horse substitute, 1 per person; Tape, 1 roll; Glow bracelets, 1 pack.

Black capes, jack-o-lanterns held at shoulder height, group gallop.

How to make it

  1. Drape the black cape over the shoulders and tuck the chin low, so you can still see the ground.
  2. Hold the pumpkin bucket up at shoulder height, like a carried head.
  3. Add glow bracelets so drivers can see you, and gallop in a group line.

Coffee Cup

⏱ 20 min hands-onEasy

You need: White trash bag or white sheet, 1 large; Cardboard tube or large cylinder, 1; Brown paper or felt for the lid, 1 sheet; Brown marker, 1 thick.

White trash bag over a cardboard tube, brown lid hat.

How to make it

  1. Cut the head and arm holes in the white bag or sheet. Cut the head hole wide enough to see and breathe with ease, and wear it over clothes.
  2. Draw a coffee shop logo on the front with the brown marker.
  3. Cut a brown paper circle as the lid hat, and wear it on top.

Classic Vampire

⏱ 25 min hands-onEasy

You need: Black cape or black bedsheet, 1; Black shirt and pants, 1 set; Plastic vampire fangs, 1 pair; Hair gel or pomade, 1; Red lipstick or red face paint, 1; White face powder or pale face paint, 1.

Black cape, fangs, slicked hair.

How to make it

  1. Dress in black, and slick the hair straight back with gel.
  2. Dust pale paint over the face, and add a thin red line at each corner of the mouth.
  3. Wear the black cape, and pop the fangs in last. Skip the fangs for small kids.

Caped Duo

⏱ 30 min hands-onEasy

You need: 2 old twin sheets or large fabric rectangles, about 4x5 feet each; Craft felt for masks and emblems, 2 sheets 9x12 inches; Ribbon or cord for cape ties, about 3 feet per cape; Fabric glue, 1 tube; Paper and pencil for sketching, 1 set; Scissors, 1 pair.

Matching sheet capes, felt masks, and your own emblems.

How to make it

  1. Cut each sheet into a cape.
  2. Cut felt emblems and eye masks. Glue one emblem to the back of each cape.
  3. Tie the capes in a loose bow that pulls free easily, and put the masks on.

Dino Herd

⏱ 30 min hands-onEasy

You need: 1 green poncho or green bedsheet per person, about 50x60 inches; 1 sheet green felt per person, 9x12 inches; 1 old sock per person, white or colored; Scrap fabric or cotton balls for stuffing, about 1 cup per person; Fabric glue, 1 tube, or 6 safety pins per person; Scissors, 1 pair.

Green ponchos with felt spikes and stuffed-sock tails.

How to make it

  1. Cut 8 to 10 felt triangles, all from one paper template.
  2. Pin the triangles in a row down the back of the green poncho.
  3. Stuff an old sock, and pin it to the back waistband as the tail.

Pixel Ghost

⏱ 30 min hands-onEasy

You need: White bedsheet or white fabric, 1 twin-size; Black felt, 1 sheet 9x12 inches; Fabric glue, 1 tube; Scissors, 1 pair; Marker and ruler, 1 each.

A white sheet cut in chunky pixel squares with felt eyes.

How to make it

  1. Cut the bottom edge of the sheet into chunky square zigzags, about 4 inches each.
  2. Draw two square eyes and a square mouth on the black felt, cut them out, and glue them onto the top third of the sheet.
  3. Press the felt pieces down firmly, one at a time. Drape the sheet on over dark clothes, with the eye area kept open.

Sushi Roll

⏱ 40 min hands-onEasy

You need: White bedsheet or large white t-shirt to wear, 1; Dark green felt, 1 large sheet; Pink or orange felt, 1 large sheet; White pom-poms, 4 large, plus 1 green pom-pom; Toy chopsticks, 1 pair, or 2 wooden sticks; Fabric glue, 1 bottle; Scissors, 1 pair.

A white-sheet wrap with felt salmon and pom-pom wasabi. Chopsticks optional.

How to make it

  1. Dress in the white sheet or large white t-shirt.
  2. Cut the dark green felt into a wide band, and glue or pin it around the waist as the seaweed wrap.
  3. Glue pink felt strips across the front as salmon, and add pom-pom rice and one green pom-pom as wasabi.

The Doctor & the Bride

⏱ 25 min hands-onEasy

You need: 1 old dark suit or jacket; Green face paint, non-toxic, 1 tube; 2 plastic neck bolts, costume style; 1 white dress or old white sheet; White hair spray, 1 can; Tape, 1 roll or glue, 1 bottle; Scissors, 1 pair; Dark eye shadow, 1.

Green face paint and neck bolts for one; tall streaked wig and torn gown for the other.

How to make it

  1. The Doctor: paint the face and neck green, and tape the two bolts to the sides of the neck. Keep paint away from the eyes, and patch-test first.
  2. The Bride: put on the white dress, and tear the bottom hem into strips by hand, starting small.
  3. Smudge dark eye shadow under the eyes. Spray in the white hair streak last.

Questions, answered

What size sheet should I use?

Twin for kids under eight, full or queen for teens and adults. A sheet that is too big drags on the ground and trips the wearer. You can always trim a sheet, you cannot add fabric back.

How do I keep a sheet ghost costume from slipping?

Cut eye holes smaller than you think first; you can widen them but not shrink them. A tied sash or belt at the waist holds the sheet in place, and safety pins at the shoulders stop it sliding on kids.

Do I need to sew anything?

No. Every costume on this page is no-sew. Cut sheet edges do not fray enough to matter for one night, and tape or pins handle the rest.

Can my child actually see through the eye holes?

Yes, if you place them right. Have the wearer put the sheet on first, mark the eye spots with a washable marker while they look straight ahead, then cut. Cut small, test, then widen.
